The Case, part 2

Yesterday morning, Mr. Novak arrived at the gallery just like every other day. He went to check on "The Midnight Moon", but it wasn't on the wall. He panicked. He remembered the blackout from the night before and immediately thought that a thief used the darkness to steal the painting.

He checked the alarm records first, but the alarm didn't work during the blackout. The alarm needs power to work, but the gallery has no backup generator. Next, he wanted to see the camera footage, but remembered that the cameras were broken. Finally, Mr. Novak called Oliver, the night guard, and asked him to report everything he saw. At first, Oliver said he watched the gallery all night and didn't fall asleep. But after a few more questions, he admitted the truth: the coffee machine was broken, and without coffee, he fell asleep around midnight.

Mr. Novak was too embarrassed to call the police, so he called his friend instead: the famous Detective Lance. Now Lance needs a witness who can answer the question: what exactly happened that night?

Grammar

Past Simple — the tense of finished actions

We use the Past Simple for actions that started and finished at a specific point in the past. It's how Mr. Novak explains exactly what he did yesterday — and how we're going to work out what really happened last night.

Part 1 — "to be": was / were

Affirmative Negative Question
I / he / she / it was wasn't Was I/he/she/it ...?
you / we / they were weren't Were you/we/they ...?
  • (+) Mr. Novak was at the restaurant last night.
  • (+) The lights were off during the blackout.
  • (-) Oliver wasn't awake at midnight.
  • (-) The cameras weren't working.
  • (?) Was the alarm broken?
  • (?) Were you at the gallery last night?

Part 2 — Verbs: did

Affirmative Negative Question
all subjects checked / fell (irregular) did not / didn't check Did ... check?
  • (+) Mr. Novak checked the alarm records.
  • (+) Oliver fell asleep around midnight. (irregular verb: fall → fell)
  • (−) The guard didn't see anyone.
  • (−) Beatrice didn't go home early.
  • (?) Did Oliver drink his coffee?
  • (?) Did Mr. Novak call the police?

Part 3 — Time expressions

Past Simple almost always comes with a time expression that tells us exactly when something happened:

yesterday · last night / week / month · two days ago · at 11:30 PM · this morning

They usually go at the end of the sentence, or at the beginning for emphasis:

  • (+) The lights went out at 11:30 PM.
  • (+) Yesterday, Mr. Novak found the painting missing.
  • (+) Beatrice left the workshop two hours ago.
  • (+) Last night, nobody heard anything unusual.

Part 4 — Wh-questions

  • Wh-words: who, what, where, when, why, how…
  • a) with "to be": Wh-word + was/were + subject?
    • Where was Mr. Novak last night?
    • Who were the suspects?
  • b) with verbs: Wh-word + did + subject + base verb?
    • What did Oliver see?
    • Why did the alarm fail?
  • c) subject questions — no "did"! When who or what is the one doing the action, we use the past tense verb directly, just like with he/she/it in the present:
    • Who took the painting? (NOT "Who did take the painting?")
    • What happened at midnight? (NOT "What did happen at midnight?")
