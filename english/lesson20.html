Reading & Vocabulary

1. Reading

Why We Forget (and How to Remember)

Learning something new is exciting, but our brain forgets new information very fast. In the 1880s, a psychologist named Hermann Ebbinghaus studied memory and discovered "the forgetting curve." He found that people forget about half of new material in just one hour. After a few days, they remember even less.

Because of this, studying for many hours the night before a test (called cramming) is one of the worst ways to learn. When you study late at night without a break, you feel tired and lose focus. You might pass the test the next morning, but a week later, you will hardly remember anything.

A much more effective method is "spaced repetition." Instead of one long study session, you review the material several times over a few days. The first review happens soon after the lesson, for example, fifteen minutes later. The second review is the next day, and the third one is three days later.

Active practice also helps a lot. Testing yourself with flashcards or explaining a topic aloud works much better than just reading your notes silently. Each time you recall a fact from memory, your memory of it gets stronger, and you remember the information for a longer time.

  2. What is "cramming," and why is it a bad way to prepare for a test?
  3. How does "spaced repetition" work? When do the first, second, and third reviews happen?
  4. Which methods work better than reading your notes silently?
  5. What happens each time you recall a fact from memory?

2. Clock Time Review: The Math Rule (+ / –)

  • 01–30 хвилин = PAST (додаємо хвилини до години, яка вже почалася):
    16:15 = a quarter past four (4 + 15 min)
    17:30 = half past five (5 + 30 min)
  • 31–59 хвилин = TO (рахуємо, скільки хвилин залишилося до наступної години):
    21:45 = a quarter to ten (10 – 15 min)
    08:50 = ten to nine (9 – 10 min)
