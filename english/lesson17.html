Grammar

Intro

Previously, Robin found a strange note while doing inventory. The note is actually a page from the Mapleton Sightseeing Guide (Mapleton is the town where Miss Lemon and Robin live). Some words on the page are erased, so Miss Lemon and Robin cannot read it. Help them fill in the missing words.

Block A: some, any and no

  • used with uncountable nouns (sugar, flour) and plural countable nouns (parks, trees)
    some sugar, some trees
  • some means “трохи/кілька” and is used in positive (+) sentences
    We need some flour for the cake.
    There are some parks in this town.
  • any is used in questions (?) and negative (-) sentences
    Is there any sugar in your tea?
    Are there any apples on this tree?
    Do you have any plans today?
    I do not have any plans today.
  • we can use no instead of not any in negative sentences
    There is no sugar in my tea.
    There are no biscuits on this plate.
  • we use some in questions (?) when we make an offer or a request
    Would you like some biscuits?
    Can I have some tea, please?
+ - ?
some not any / no any / some

Block B: a lot of, much and many

  • a lot of means “багато” and is used in positive (+) sentences with uncountable and plural countable nouns
    There is a lot of bread on that shelf.
    There are a lot of apples on that plate.
    We have a lot of homework today.
  • we drop of if there's no noun after a lot
    She studies a lot.
    They travel a lot.
  • much and many also mean “багато”, but they are usually used in questions (?) and negations (-). Much is used with uncountable nouns and many – with countable nouns
    Is there much sugar in this recipe? We don't have much sugar left.
    Are there many apples in this recipe? We don't have many apples.
  • we use how much and how many to ask “Скільки?”
    How much homework do you have today?
    How much does this cake cost?
    How many parks are there in Mapleton?
    How many years has she worked here?
  • too many and too much are used to say there's “надто багато” of something
    There are too many people in this park.
    There is too much noise.
+ - ?
countable a lot of many (how) many
uncountable a lot of much (how) much

Block C: few, little, a few and a little

  • we use few and little to say “мало / небагато / майже немає”. few is used with plural countable nouns, and little is used with uncountable nouns
    There are few visitors in the library today.
    We have little time until we need to close the library.
  • we can use very for emphasis
    We have very few biscuits and very little tea. That's not enough for all of us.
  • we use a few and a little to say “трохи / кілька”. Their meaning is similar to some
    We have a few apples. We can make some apple juice.
    I have a little time. I can read one more chapter of this book.
  • compare few/little and a few/a little:
    I have few apples, so I can't bake an apple pie.
    I have a few apples, so I'll use them to bake an apple pie.
    I have little time, so I can't finish my homework.
    I have a little time, so I can finish my homework.
  • we can also use a little before adjectives and adverbs to say “трохи / дещо”. You can say a bit instead of a little. We don't use few or little (without a) in this way
    It is a little cold today. It is a bit cold today.
    I feel a little tired.
    She speaks a little slowly.
трохи мало дуже мало
countable a few few very few
uncountable a little little very little

Puzzle

Questions about the texts

At the bottom of the note, someone wrote five questions in pencil. Miss Lemon reads them slowly. "This is not an ordinary guide," she says. "It looks like a piece of a puzzle."

Answer each question with a full sentence (say it aloud). Use the grammar from today's lesson. Then write down the short answer to the first question in each pair in the box below (1 or 2 words, without a / an / the at the start). Write each answer on a new line.

  1. Which place on Maple Street has some beautiful things? Are there many of them?
  2. What do people have instead of facts about the secret book club? Do many people know about this club?
  3. In which season does the whole town look like a painting? Is there much colour in the streets during this season?
  4. What do the students study at Mapleton University, besides maths? How much free time do they have?
  5. A sign in Miss Snow's bakery offers you something to drink. What is it? Make the offer using this drink's name: "Would you like ...?"

Look at the first letters of your five short answers, in order. They make a word. What is it?
